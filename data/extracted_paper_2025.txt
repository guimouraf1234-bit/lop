ABSTRACT 
This work investigates the leaching kinetics of roasted zinc in aqueous solutions of 
sulphuric acid from an electrolytic zinc production plant. Parameters such as stirring 
speed, temperature, granulometry, and acid concentration were investigated aiming to 
model the experimental data according to the shrinking core and progressive conversion 
models. The kinetic study determined that leaching of the roasted concentrate follows the 
progressive conversion model and is controlled by the chemical reaction step, whose 
activation energy, enthalpy, a nd apparent entropy values are 13.45 kJ.mol -1, 
11.07 kJ.mol-1 and -256.20 J.mol-1.K-1, respectively. The estimated value of the chemical 
reaction rate constant (ks) is 3.00 x 10-2 cm.s-1. 
 
Keywords: Kinetic Study, Roasted Zinc Concentrate, Shrinking Core Model, Progressive 
Conversion Model, Leaching.

1 INTRODUCTION 
 
The dissolution of ZnO in sulphuric acid solution has been investigated by several 
authors from both practical and theoretical viewpoints (Bortot Coelho et al., 2020; Coelho 
et al., 2018; Kashyap and Taylor, 2022; USGS, 2025). Jandová et al. (1999) investigated 
the leaching behaviour of several ZnO compounds obtained by calcination of zinc 
hydroxides produced by precipitation of ZnSO 4 solutions. They concluded that ZnO 
leaching in diluted H 2SO4 solutions is controlled by diffusion processes. Terry (1983) 
established correlations involving chemical reaction rates of ZnO, Zn2SiO4 and ZnFe2O4 
individual species and of the different mixture of these compounds in aqueous solutions 
of H2SO4. Doyle et al. (1987) developed a mathematical model to describe the leaching 
kinetics of ZnO in diluted sulphuric acid solutions considering that the mass transport 
controls the dissolution rate. 
Most studies that seek to understand the dissolution mechanisms use pure species 
or a mixture of synthetic compounds. However, the approach involving dissolution aspect 
of roasted zinc concentrates in sulphuric medium obtained in industrial scale is scarce and 
is relevant as 80% of the zinc presently produced in the world is pr oduced by roasting-
leaching-electrolysis using zinc sulphide concentrates and, in some cases, associated with 
the leaching of zinc silicates (Chen and Dutrizac, 2004; Harmer et al., 2007; Souza et al., 
2007; Balarini et al., 2008).

The literature presents several kinetic models that describe the reaction between a 
solid and a fluid. Two of these models are largely used and are simple to develop – the 
shrinking core model, and the progressive conversion model ( LEVENSPIEL, 1974). 
They separate the leaching pr ocess in two large determining steps: the surface chemical 
reaction and the mass transport between the fluid and the solid. Considering these two 
models, this work aimed to study the leaching kinetics of roasted zinc concentrates in 
diluted solutions of sulphuric acid to determine the process-determining step and estimate 
the kinetic and thermodynamic parameters, such as specific chemical reaction rate, 
activation energy, apparent enthalpy, and entropy.

2.2 LEACHING EXPERIMENTS 
 
The discontinued leaching tests were performed in a 500 -mL glass reactor with 
6.5 cm i.d.  and 15 cm high, an external jacket for the control of the temperature and a 
condenser couple to the lid to prevent water vapour loss. The stirring system was con-
trolled with a mechanical agitator with digital control and a glass impeller with two 
pitched blades at 60°. The temperature range investigated was 30-70 °C and the sulphuric 
acid concentrations ranged from 4 g.L -1 to 10 g.L-1. The mass of the roasted concentrate 
was calculated considering an acid consumption at the end of the experiments limited t o 
10% of its mass. At set intervals, reacted samples were taken, vacuum filtered, and diluted 
to prevent the chemical reaction continuing. The zinc extraction was determined by 
atomic absorption spectrophotometry in Thermo Electron Corporation, M Series. T hese 
results allowed calculating the percentage of zinc extraction as a function of time.

From Equation 3, one can estimate the concentration of the leaching solution (C’A)

The preliminary experiments showed that for a concentration of CA = 20 g.L-1, the

After determining the time for the maximum conversion of 5 min, a time of 10

expand the initial leaching period, which allow a better mathematical modelling of the 
kinetic equations.

3.3 THE EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE 
 
The dependence on the temperature may be used to estimate the activation energy 
and the apparent activation enthalpy and activation entropy (Dreisinger and Abed, 2002).

The activation energy is an extremely useful  parameter for the identification of the pro-
cess determining step; however, this is not consensual. According to Prosser (1996), val-
ues lower than 25 kJ.mol-1 characterize a mass transfer-controlled process (through fluid 
and/or product layer) and processe s controlled by chemical reaction may have either 
lower or higher activation energy values. According to  Levenspiel (1972) apud 
Dreisinger and Abed (2002), it is widely accepted that systems with activation energy 
higher than 40 kJ.mol-1 are controlled by chemical reaction (linear leaching), while those 
with activation energy less than 40 kJ.mol -1 are controlled by mass transport processes 
(parabolic leaching), whether in the product layer or a boundary fluid film. 
Figure 5(a) shows that the temperature has a discrete effect on the leaching curves 
and that a small increase in the extraction rate (curve slope) can be noted at one min, when 
the temperature rises from 40 to 50°C and from 60 to 70°C. Furthermore, one can notice 
that all the cu rves, with the exception of that of 30°C, level off after 3  min of reaction. 
The slight difference between this curve and the others after 3 min of reaction and the 
small increase in the extraction observed at one minute are within the experimental errors, 
as shown in Figure 5(b). This figure also shows that the higher temperature curve (70 °C) 
also have comparatively larger experimental errors. This high data dispersion for 70 °C 
is due to the difficulty in controlling the system at this temperature. Despite the use of the 
condenser and jacket, the water vapour and heat losses increase with the increase in tem-
perature.

The lack of significant differences in the reaction rate with the increase in the 
temperature shown in Figure 5 seems to indicate that mass transfer may be the process 
controlling step. One way to verify this hypothesis is to use theoretical kinetic models. 
Figures 5(a) and 5(b) display the linearization curves of the shrinking core model consid-
ering the diffusion in the product layer and the chemical reaction as the determining steps, 
respectively. 
From the analysis of the determination coefficients (R2) and of the curves in Fig-
ure 6, we can see that the data demonstrate a slightly better fitting for the control by the 
diffusion in the product layer than for the chemical reaction control. However, the pre-
sented linearizations are not sufficient to define  the controlling step of the investigated 
system. The study of other variables, such as granulometry, acid concentration, which 
will be discussed below, are also required. Until other parameters are evaluated, both 
steps will be considered as controlling the leaching process.

estimated value of the chemical reaction rate constant (ks) was 3.00 x 10-2 cm.s-1.
